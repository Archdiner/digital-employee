Use when comparing actual results to budget, forecast, or prior period, or when asked "why are we off plan".

# Budget versus actual

## Set up the comparison before touching numbers
1. Fix the three labels: period (month, quarter, YTD), basis (actual vs budget, vs latest forecast, vs prior year), and level (entity, business unit, cost centre). Write them at the top of the output. Most wrong answers come from mixing a YTD actual with a full-year budget, or the original budget with a re-forecast.
2. Confirm the budget version. Firms keep the approved budget, a re-based budget after reorganisation, and rolling forecasts. Use the one the firm notes name; if they do not, ask.
3. Confirm currency and unit from the source headers. A pack in AED 000s and a budget in USD m is a common trap.

## Build the bridge
- For each line: actual, budget, variance in absolute terms, variance in percent. Both always. A 40% variance on a small line is noise; a 2% variance on revenue is the story.
- Sign convention: favourable is positive for revenue and negative for costs, unless the firm's template does the opposite. Follow the template.
- Rank by absolute variance, not by line order. The reader's first question is "what are the three things that matter".
- Split volume and price/rate effects only when the source gives both drivers. Do not back-solve one from the other.

## Explain
- Take drivers from the commentary in the pack, the cost-centre owner's note, or the prior variance report. Quote the source. If no explanation exists, write "no explanation in the pack" and list it as an open item.
- Distinguish timing (phasing, accruals, a delayed invoice) from permanent (a lost customer, a rate change). Timing reverses; permanent changes the full-year view.
- Recommendations follow from permanent variances only. State the action, the owner if named in the sources, and the number it would move.

## Never
- Never call a variance "immaterial" without the firm's threshold from the firm notes (default 5% and any sign change).
- Never restate the budget to make a variance disappear.
