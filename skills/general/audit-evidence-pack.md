Use when preparing documents for auditors, answering an audit request list, or supporting audit fieldwork.

# Audit evidence pack

## Principle
Auditors want the source, not your summary. Your job is to find, label and reconcile, never to explain away.

## Steps
1. Take the request list as the index. One line per request: reference number, what is asked, period, entity, owner, due date, status.
2. For each item, locate the primary document: ledger extract, invoice, contract, bank statement, board minute, calculation schedule. Record where it came from (system, folder, file, page).
3. Reconcile every schedule to the ledger or the reported figure before it goes out. A schedule that does not tie is a finding waiting to happen. Show the tie in a cover line: "Agrees to TB account 4100 at 31 Dec, difference nil".
4. Name files by the request reference so the auditor can match them without asking.
5. Keep a log of what was sent, when, and to whom. That log is the answer to "did we already give you this".

## Prior-year context
Read last year's management letter points and the prior year's request list. Items raised before will be raised again; prepare them first.

## When something cannot be found
Say so on the tracker with what was searched. Do not substitute a similar document. Do not reconstruct a figure to fill the gap.

## Never
- Never alter a source document. Annotations go on a cover sheet.
- Never send a draft schedule without marking it draft.
- Never answer a question about accounting judgement; route it to the named reviewer in the firm notes.
