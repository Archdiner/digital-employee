Use when preparing or updating a forecast, re-forecast, outlook, or full-year estimate.

# Forecast update

## What a forecast is made of
- Actuals to date, taken from the fact store, never re-typed.
- Remaining-period assumptions: run-rate, seasonality from prior years, known one-offs, management's stated plans.
- The method, stated in one line at the top: "Q3-Q4 at Q2 run-rate adjusted for the August shutdown" is a method. "Management estimate" is not.

## Steps
1. Anchor on the latest closed period. Say which one.
2. Pull the same period last year and the prior forecast for the same horizon. The forecast is judged against both.
3. Build the remaining periods line by line with the compute tool. Every projected figure is a computed fact with a formula, so the reader can see "Q4 revenue = Q2 actual × 1.04 seasonal factor from FY25".
4. Reconcile: actuals to date plus forecast remainder equals the full-year figure. Show it.
5. Compare the full-year figure to budget and to the prior forecast. Explain the movement since the prior forecast; that movement is what management reads.
6. Sensitivities only where the firm asks: one variable at a time, the base case unchanged.

## Say what kind of number each one is
Mark actuals, forecasts and assumptions distinctly in every table. A reader must never mistake a projection for a result.

## Never
- Never extrapolate a trend from fewer than three periods without saying so.
- Never smooth a one-off into the run-rate. Take it out, footnote it.
- Never present a single-point forecast for a line the source describes as uncertain without the range the source gives.
