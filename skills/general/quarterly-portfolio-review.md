Use when asked to write, draft, or update a quarterly portfolio review for a fund or a set of portfolio companies.

# Quarterly portfolio review

## Before writing
1. Read the firm notes. They say which funds and companies exist, what terms they use, and where the numbers live.
2. Read the most recent past review, if there is one. Match its layout, section order, table shapes, length, and tone. Do not invent a new format.
3. Search the fact store for every company and metric the past review covers, for the current quarter, the prior quarter, and the same quarter last year.
4. If a number the review needs is not in the fact store, do not estimate it. Note it as missing and either ask or leave a clearly marked gap.

## Standard shape (use the past review's shape if it differs)
- Title: fund name, quarter, "Quarterly Portfolio Review".
- Portfolio summary: one paragraph on the quarter, then a table with one row per company: revenue, EBITDA, margin, net debt, and the quarter-on-quarter change.
- One section per company: two to four short paragraphs. What happened, why, what management expects. One table per company if the past review had one.
- Watch list: companies behind plan or with covenant, liquidity, or leadership issues.
- Open items: anything the reviewer should know you were unsure about.

## Numbers
- Every number comes from the fact store, cited by fact id. Never type a number from memory.
- Changes (growth, margin movement, variance to plan) are computed with the compute tool from stored facts, never in your head.
- Keep the source's units and precision. Do not round a figure the source did not round.

## Tone
- Plain, direct, past tense for what happened, present tense for the current position.
- No adjectives that do not carry information ("strong", "robust", "solid"). Say the number and what drove it.
- Short sentences. One idea each.
