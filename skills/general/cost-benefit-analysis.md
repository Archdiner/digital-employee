Use when assessing a proposed project, investment, system, hire, or process change in financial terms.

# Cost-benefit analysis

## Frame it
1. State the decision in one sentence and the alternative it is compared against (usually "do nothing", which also has a cost).
2. Fix the horizon, the currency, and whether figures are nominal or real. Take discount or hurdle rates from the firm notes; if absent, ask rather than assume.

## Costs and benefits
- List every cost: one-off, recurring, internal time, transition, exit. Each with a source: a quote, a contract, a comparable from the fact store.
- List benefits the same way, separated into cash benefits (savings, revenue) and non-cash (risk reduction, compliance). Only cash benefits enter the numbers; non-cash go in words.
- Timing per period, then the compute tool for the net position per period, cumulative, payback period, and NPV at the firm's rate. Every figure a computed fact.

## Test it
- Which single assumption, if wrong by a plausible amount, flips the answer? Show that case.
- What is the cost of delay by one period, if the sources allow it to be computed?

## Present
Decision, recommendation with the one number that carries it, the table of periods, the sensitivity, the assumptions with sources, the open items. One page unless the firm's template differs.

## Never
- Never include a benefit no source supports. "Expected efficiency gains" without a number and an owner is not a benefit.
- Never present NPV without the rate and horizon beside it.
